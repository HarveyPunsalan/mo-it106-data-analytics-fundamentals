## The charts

![MotorPH Products by Category and Units Sold](MotorPH_Category_Chart.png)

## Chart type

I used two bar charts stacked in one image. The top chart shows the product count per category, with the count and its share of all 50 products on each bar, for example 12 (24%). The bottom chart shows the units sold per category from the sales data. Putting them side by side makes it easy to see where the shelf (what we carry) and the sales (what customers buy) agree or disagree.

## Key insights

Scooter leads on both sides with 12 products (24%) and 4,036 units sold, so the count and the demand agree there. But the rankings shift below it. Cruiser and Sport both carry 4 models and sell almost the same units (1,427 vs 1,425), yet Cruiser earned P635M against Sport's P372M, so the count ranking hides a real revenue gap. Heritage moves up the most: 9th by units sold (702) but 4th by revenue (P553M) from only 2 models. Commuter goes the other way: 7th in units (902) but near the bottom in revenue (P53M) because the bikes are cheap. And the fastest movers per model are the single-model categories: Street sold 482 units from 1 model, Classic 458 from 1, and Commuter 451 per model, all faster than Scooter's 336 per model.

Therefore product count alone does not prove demand, because the shelf ranking and the sales ranking are different lists. A category can look small on the shelf and still earn big (Heritage), or look busy in units and earn little (Commuter). As a result, restocking should be based on units sold and revenue per category, not on how many models sit in each group. Scooters still deserve priority since they lead in count, units, and revenue, but single-model fast movers like Street and Classic should not be starved just because their category has fewer models.
